Child Labour & Exploitation Awareness

🏫

Real-Life Example

A school-age child is made to work for long hours in a dangerous workplace instead of attending school. The child is not given adequate rest or a safe environment.

🛑 What Is It?

Child labour refers to work that is harmful to children, interferes with their education, or negatively affects their physical, mental, social, or emotional development.

⚡ How Does It Work?

Children may be exploited when adults or businesses take advantage of their vulnerability and make them work under unsafe, unfair, or forced conditions.

🎭 Exploiter's Tactics / False Promises
  • Promising a child or family good wages but providing little or no payment.
  • Promising education or skills training but instead forcing the child to work.
  • Using threats, intimidation, or fear to control children.
  • Hiding dangerous, unsanitary, or illegal working conditions.
  • Preventing children from leaving the workplace or seeking outside help.
🚩 Red Flags (Clear Warning Signs)
  • A child is regularly absent from school or dropped out because of work.
  • A child is forced to work excessively long hours without rest.
  • The work involves hazardous machinery, chemicals, or physically harmful tasks.
  • The child appears frightened, threatened, malnourished, or strictly controlled.
  • A child is denied proper food, healthcare, rest, education, or basic safety.
  • Someone is actively preventing the child from contacting family or seeking help.

🛡️ How to Stay Safe & Prevent Exploitation

1

Prioritize Education

Children should remain actively connected to school, learning environments, and trusted educators.

2

Verify Job Offers

Never accept job or travel opportunities without a trusted adult thoroughly checking them first.

3

Protect Official Documents

Do not hand over identity papers, personal documents, or sensitive details to employment agents.

4

Report Threatening Behavior

Tell a trusted adult immediately if someone is forcing, threatening, or coercing a child to work.

5

Community Vigilance

Communities should actively advocate for and support every child's right to education and protection.

🚨 What to Do If It Happens

1

Inform Trusted Authorities

Tell a parent, school teacher, counselor, child-protection worker, or trusted adult immediately.

2

Contact Emergency Response

If a child is in immediate physical danger, contact emergency law enforcement or child safety services.

3

Dial Child Helpline 1098

In India, report concerns about any child needing care, rescue, or protection via Childline 1098.

📞

Know a Child Facing Exploitation or Forced Labour?

Report child abuse and exploitation immediately to government child protection services.

📞 Child Helpline: 1098 📞 Emergency Response: 112 📞 Police: 100
