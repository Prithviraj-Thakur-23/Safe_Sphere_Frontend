Stranger Danger & Unsafe Approaches

🚸

Real-Life Example

A child is walking home from school when an unknown person approaches and says, “Your parents sent me to pick you up. Come with me.” The child does not know the person and has not received confirmation from their parents.

🛑 What Is It?

Stranger danger means being cautious when an unfamiliar person approaches, especially when they ask a child to go somewhere, keep a secret, or provide personal information.

⚡ How Does It Work?

An unsafe person may try to gain a child's trust through friendly conversation, gifts, food, money, promises, or by pretending to know the child's family.

🎭 Common Tricks Used by Unsafe People
  • Claiming: “Your mother/father asked me to pick you up.”
  • Offering toys, sweets, money, or a free ride.
  • Pretending to be a close family friend or neighbor.
  • Asking the child to help them find a lost pet or object.
  • Asking the child to keep their conversation or meeting a secret.
🚩 Red Flags (Clear Warning Signs)
  • An unknown person asks you to go somewhere alone with them.
  • Someone asks for your home address, phone number, or school details.
  • Someone tells you to keep a secret from your parents or teachers.
  • Someone makes you feel scared, pressured, or uncomfortable.
  • Someone tries to separate you from your parent or trusted adult.

🛡️ How to Stay Safe

1

Never Go Along

Never go anywhere with an unfamiliar person without explicit permission from a trusted adult.

2

Stay in Public Areas

Always stay in bright, public, and well-populated places where others can see you.

3

Protect Personal Details

Do not share personal information, home address, or school details with strangers.

4

Use Safety Passwords

Use a secret family-approved safety password if someone claims your parents sent them.

5

Say NO & Move Away

Say a firm “NO!”, quickly move away, and seek help immediately if you feel unsafe.

🚨 What to Do If It Happens

1

Move to Safety

Run or walk quickly toward a safe, crowded, or well-lit area immediately.

2

Tell a Trusted Adult

Inform a parent, teacher, school staff, security guard, or police officer right away.

3

Shout Loudly

If someone tries to take you by force, shout loudly: "HELP! THIS IS NOT MY PARENT!"

4

Remember Key Details

Try to remember details about the person (clothing, vehicle, direction they went).

📞

Need Immediate Help or Assistance for a Child?

Contact national emergency and child protection helplines available 24/7.

📞 Childline: 1098 📞 Emergency Response: 112 📞 Police: 100
