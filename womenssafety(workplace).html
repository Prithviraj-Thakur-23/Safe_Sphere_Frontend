College, Workplace and Emergency Safety

🏫

Real-Life Example

A female student stays late in the college laboratory to complete her project. While returning alone, an unknown person follows her and makes her uncomfortable. She moves to a crowded area and contacts a trusted person.

🏢 What Is It?

Women's safety at college and the workplace means protecting women from harassment, stalking, unsafe environments, inappropriate behaviour, accidents and other risks.

⚙️ How Does It Work?
  • Women may spend time in classrooms, laboratories, offices and workshops.
  • Unsafe environments or isolated areas can increase risk.
  • Harassment or stalking may occur unexpectedly.
  • Following safety procedures and knowing emergency contacts helps reduce risk.
  • Reporting unsafe behaviour helps authorities take action.
💬 Unsafe Practices / Statements

Unsafe individuals may attempt to isolate or manipulate you using phrases like:

  • "Don't tell anyone about this."
  • "Come with me, I will help you."
  • "Your friend asked me to pick you up."
  • "Don't worry, this is normal."
  • "Give me your phone; I'll make the call."
🚩 Red Flags – Clear Warning Signs
  • Someone repeatedly follows you.
  • Unwanted messages or calls continue after you say no.
  • Someone waits around your workplace or college regularly.
  • A person pressures you to meet privately.
  • Unsafe or poorly lit areas within or around premises.

🛡️ How to Stay Safe

1

Maintain Surroundings Awareness

Stay alert and aware of your environment at all times while in classrooms, labs, offices, or transit paths.

2

Avoid Isolated Areas

Steer clear of deserted corridors, unlit stairwells, or empty campus labs when working late or alone.

3

Keep Emergency Contacts Ready

Maintain fast access to institutional security, internal complaint committees, and emergency phone numbers.

4

Share Travel & Work Plans

Inform trusted family members, colleagues, or friends whenever working extra hours or traveling alone.

5

Report Harassment Early

Promptly report any stalking, unwanted behavior, or boundary violations to supervisors or campus authorities.

🚨 What to Do If It Happens

1

Move to Populated Area

Head immediately towards a safe, brightly lit, and crowded public location like a canteen, library, or security desk.

2

Contact Trusted Person

Reach out to a family member, trusted friend, or colleague to inform them of your exact location and situation.

3

Inform Authorities

Notify faculty members, workplace supervisors, campus security guards, or appropriate institutional bodies immediately.

4

Preserve All Evidence

Save screenshots of unwanted messages, call logs, photographs, or any other relevant evidence of harassment.

📞

Facing Emergency Harassment or Institutional Threat?

Call government and institutional safety helplines directly for urgent 24/7 assistance.

📞 Emergency: 112 📞 Women Helpline: 1091 📞 Women Distress: 181
