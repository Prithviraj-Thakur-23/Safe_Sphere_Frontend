Fake Scholarship & Education Scams Guide

A fake scholarship scam is a fraud in which criminals pretend to offer scholarships, educational benefits, admission opportunities, or government schemes in order to steal money or personal information.

🚨 Real-Life Examples

  • A student receives a fake message saying they have received a ₹50,000 scholarship.
  • A website asks students to pay a ₹500 registration fee for a government scholarship.
  • Someone calls pretending to be from a college and asks for bank details to release scholarship money.
  • A fake website uses a government logo to collect students' government ID and bank information.
  • A Telegram group promises a 100% guaranteed scholarship after paying a membership fee.
  • A scammer sends a fake scholarship selection letter and asks for money to confirm admission.

⚙️ How It Works

  • The scammer calls or messages the student claiming to offer a scholarship.
  • They inform the student that they have been selected for financial aid.
  • They send a fake application form or a deceptive website link.
  • The student is asked to provide identification documents, bank account details, and mobile numbers.
  • The scammer asks for an OTP to "verify" the application.
  • They demand a registration, processing, or verification fee before funds are released.
  • After receiving money or information, they vanish and the promised scholarship never arrives.

🎭 Scammer's Tricks

  • Using forged government logos and trusted university names.
  • Issuing convincing fake selection letters.
  • Creating professional-looking fraudulent websites.
  • Creating intense urgency by claiming applications close within a few hours.

🚩 Major Red Flags

  • Guaranteed scholarship offers without proper eligibility criteria.
  • Unexpected selection messages for scholarships you never applied for.
  • Requests for registration, processing, or verification fees upfront.
  • Suspicious website domains or application links.
  • Poor spelling, grammar, or unusual language in communications.
  • Requests for OTPs, PINs, or account passwords.
  • Scholarships that cannot be verified on official government or university websites.

🛡️ How to Stay Safe

  • Verify scholarship information through official government or institution websites.
  • Do not trust messages or offers received from unknown phone numbers.
  • Never share OTPs, PINs, passwords, or banking credentials with anyone.
  • Do not pay any individual or third party to release a scholarship.

⚡ What to Do If It Happens

  • Stop all communication with the suspected scammer immediately.
  • Save copies of messages, website links, phone numbers, and payment details.
  • If money has been transferred, contact your bank immediately.
  • Report the incident through the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal.
