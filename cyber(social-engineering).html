Cyber Security Threat Advisory

Social Engineering

Social engineering involves psychological manipulation where scammers trick individuals into voluntarily sharing confidential information, transferring funds, or bypassing security controls.

Real-Life Scenario

The Fake Bank Call

You receive a call from someone claiming to be a bank representative stating there is an urgent security issue with your account. They request your One-Time Password (OTP) to resolve it. Trusting their authoritative tone, you share the OTP, which the scammer immediately exploits to access your account or authorize unauthorized transactions.

Understanding the Threat

What Is It?

Manipulative tactics where fraudsters deceive people into giving up sensitive data or performing actions that help compromise accounts. Instead of hacking systems directly, they exploit human trust.

How It Works

Scammers contact victims via phone, SMS, email, or social media posing as trusted authorities. By manufactured fear, excitement, or false urgency, they pressure victims into yielding critical information.

Common Scammer Disguises & Tactics

Scammers commonly impersonate trusted authorities or acquaintances:

🏦 Bank Employees
👮 Police Officers
🏛️ Government Officials
🎧 Customer-Care Staff
👥 Friends or Relatives
📦 Delivery Agents

Psychological Triggers

Fraudsters deliberately leverage fear, trust, greed, or extreme urgency to make you act instantly without pausing to think critically.

Red Flags (Clear Warning Signs)

  • Someone asks directly for your sensitive OTP, PIN, or account password.
  • The caller creates unnecessary panic, haste, or pressure.
  • You are threatened with immediate account blocking, legal action, or arrest.
  • A caller instructs you to download and install a remote-access or verification app.
  • Someone requests a money transfer to “verify” or “test” your banking account.
  • The person refuses or avoids letting you independently verify their identity or official credentials.
  • You receive unexpected, unusual money requests or links from friends or relatives.

How to Stay Safe

Stop and Think

Pause before responding to any unexpected communication demanding action or urgency.

Never Share Credentials

Never disclose OTPs, PINs, or passwords to anyone, regardless of who they claim to be.

Verify Independently

Verify unexpected calls or alerts using official customer service numbers found on bank cards or authorized portals rather than callbacks.

Emergency Action Steps

1. Cut Communication

Stop talking or messaging with the scammer immediately and do not provide any further details.

2. Secure Accounts (1930)

Change compromised passwords right away, contact your bank to freeze transactions, and call 1930 if financial losses occurred.

3. Report & Save Evidence

File a formal complaint on cybercrime.gov.in and preserve chat logs, phone numbers, and screenshots.

Official Portals & Emergency Resources:
  • National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal: https://www.cybercrime.gov.in/
  • Cyber Crime Helpline: Call 1930 for financial cyber fraud reporting.
  • Emergency Police Helpline: Call 112 for immediate crisis assistance.
