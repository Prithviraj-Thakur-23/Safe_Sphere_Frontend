Technical Support Fraud

Fake Customer-Care Scams Dashboard

A scam where criminals publish fraudulent contact numbers on search engines, maps, and social media to pose as customer support representatives. They exploit users facing technical or service issues, directing them toward malicious screen-sharing apps or payment links.

🚨 Real-Life Scammer Tactics & Scenarios

🍔 Food Delivery Refund

"After an order gets canceled, searching online for help leads to a fake agent instructing you to install AnyDesk to 'verify the refund wallet'."

✈️ Travel Ticket Cancellation

"Trying to reschedule a flight, a traveler reaches a scammer who sends a link to pay a ₹10 'modification fee', draining their account."

💸 Failed UPI Transaction

"After a UPI payment gets stuck, calling a social media helpline results in instructions to open your UPI app and enter your PIN to 'receive the reversed payment'."

📦 Courier Clearance Fee

"A customer waiting for an overseas courier is directed by fake customer support to deposit customs clearance charges into a private account."

⚙️ How Does the Scam Work?

1

SEO Manipulation: Scammers create fake listings on search engines, social media, and online maps using reputable brand names.

2

The Inbound Call: A customer encountering an issue searches the internet and dials the fraudulent number.

3

Remote App Installation: The fake agent claims they need remote access and instructs you to install AnyDesk, TeamViewer, or QuickSupport.

4

Credential Harvesting: Once remote access is granted, they observe your password, PIN, and OTP directly on their screen.

5

Fund Transfer: The scammer executes unauthorized transactions using the gathered credentials.

🎭 Scammer Mind Tricks

  • SEO Poisoning: Pushing fake numbers to top search engine results so they appear genuine.
  • Technical Jargon: Disguising remote access apps as 'Official Company Support Toolkits' or 'Secure Refund Servers'.
  • Exploiting Frustration: Taking advantage of a customer's urgency and panic to resolve an existing problem quickly.

🚩 Major Red Flags

  • Support numbers listed as normal 10-digit mobile numbers (+91-9XXXXXXXXX) rather than official 1800 toll-free numbers.
  • Support agents asking you to download screen-sharing or remote management applications.
  • Being asked to pay a small 'verification fee' or 'processing charge' to receive a refund.
  • Being asked to scan a QR code or enter your UPI PIN to receive money.

🛡️ Defense & Safety Rules

  • Use In-App Support Only: Never search public search engines for support numbers; use built-in 'Help & Support' tabs.
  • Check Verified Channels: Look for blue verification ticks on official brand accounts and secure HTTPS web links.
  • Never Install Remote Tools: Refuse all requests from support staff to install screen-sharing software.
  • Verify Toll-Free Numbers: Genuine corporate helplines usually operate through official toll-free numbers starting with 1800.

⚡ What to Do If Compromised

  • Cut Network Connections: Put your phone on Airplane Mode and disconnect from Wi-Fi immediately to terminate active remote sessions.
  • Uninstall Remote Apps: Delete AnyDesk, TeamViewer, or any newly downloaded apps/files immediately.
  • Lock Banking Accounts: Contact your bank from another device to freeze your accounts, cards, and UPI access.
  • File a Cybercrime Report: Dial 1930 immediately and register your complaint online.

🚨 Emergency Helpline & Reporting Channels

1930 National Cyber Crime Helpline
1915 National Consumer Helpline (NCH)
112 National Emergency Number
