Good Touch, Bad Touch & Body Safety

🛡️

Real-Life Example

A child is uncomfortable when someone touches them or asks them to keep an uncomfortable interaction secret. The child says “Stop,” moves away, and tells a trusted adult.

💡 What Is It?

Body safety teaches children that their bodies belong to them and that they have the right to feel safe and set boundaries about physical contact.

🔄 How Does It Work?

Children learn to recognize comfortable and uncomfortable situations, understand personal boundaries, say “No,” move away from unsafe situations, and tell a trusted adult.

🎭 Unsafe Person's / Scammer's Tricks
  • Pretending that inappropriate behavior is just a fun game.
  • Offering gifts, rewards, or special privileges to gain trust.
  • Using threats, intimidation, or creating fear.
  • Insisting: “This is our special secret.”
  • Attempting to make the child feel responsible or guilty.
🚩 Red Flags (Clear Warning Signs)
  • Someone touches private areas or asks a child to touch theirs.
  • Someone asks a child to keep uncomfortable touching a secret.
  • Someone shows inappropriate or uncomfortable pictures or videos.
  • A child feels scared, confused, pressured, or uncomfortable.
  • Someone ignores a child's explicit request to stop.

🛑 How to Stay Safe

1

Know Your Body Belongs to You

Understand that your body is yours alone and you have the right to decide who touches you.

2

Say "NO" or "STOP"

Firmly refuse and speak up whenever a touch makes you feel uncomfortable, confused, or unsafe.

3

Move Away to Safety

Immediately step away from the unsafe situation and go to a safe place with other people.

4

Tell a Trusted Adult

Share what happened with a parent, teacher, relative, or another adult you trust.

5

Keep Telling Until Helped

If the first adult doesn't listen or take action, keep telling other trusted adults until someone helps you.

❤️ Remember: It is NEVER the child's fault.

🚨 What to Do If It Happens

1

Get to a Safe Location

Get away from the unsafe person or situation as quickly and safely as possible.

2

Report Immediately

Tell a parent, teacher, school counselor, or another trusted adult right away.

3

Do Not Blame Yourself

Never feel guilty or blame yourself for someone else's inappropriate behavior.

4

Seek Emergency Help

If in immediate danger, contact emergency services or seek help from nearby trusted adults.

📞

Need Immediate Help or Protection?

Reach out to official emergency services and child protection helplines anytime.
